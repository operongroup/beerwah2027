Weeks 1 to 6: the catch-up

Everything worth saying about the part you did not film.

You have almost no footage from the first six weeks. That is fine, and you should not fake it. The fix is one honest piece to camera that covers the ground, then you start filming properly from week 7.

How to use this

Film one sit-down piece, six to eight minutes, in a single take. Webcam at the Mac mini, DJI mic on. Work down this page week by week. Each week has a headline, the real numbers, one or two lines to say out loud, and a closing beat.

Do not script it. Read a week, look away from the screen, say it in your own words. The numbers are here so you get them right, not so you read them off.

Cut it later into six shorts, one per week, if you want something to post while week 7 is being filmed.

Week 127 July to 2 August

A baseline, then the flu

Run18.6km
Sessions4 of 5
Baseline 5km27:46

Monday's baseline time trial: 5.01km in 27:46, 5:33/km, average heart rate 169, at about 90% effort. Every zone in the plan was set from that one run. Tuesday's easy run felt so easy he stretched it from 3km to 4km. Wednesday he ran 3.55km and then the flu arrived. Thursday was lost. Saturday he ran 6km anyway at about 85% recovered.

Say this
"Week one I did a time trial to find out where I actually was. Twenty-seven forty-six for five kilometres. My personal best is twenty-two twenty-nine. That gap is the whole show."
"Then I got the flu on day three. Six months of training and I lost the first week."
Closing beat: the plan does not care that you started badly. It just moves on.
Week 23 to 9 August

A birthday run and a race that was not supposed to be a race

Run22.1km
Sessions4 of 4
Rainbow Beach10.42km

Monday, an unplanned birthday run: 3.5km at 5:26/km, heart rate 171. Faster than his baseline time trial pace, on a whim, on his birthday. Saturday was the Rainbow Beach 11km trail festival, entered for enjoyment and explicitly not to be raced. He ran it at an average heart rate of 178, which is 89% of maximum, and it came back as the highest training load of the entire block so far.

Say this
"Rainbow Beach was supposed to be a fun run. My average heart rate was one seventy-eight. I raced it. I just did not admit it at the time."
"That is the thing about having the data. You cannot tell yourself a story about how easy it felt when the file says otherwise."
Closing beat: the first time the numbers disagreed with him, and they were right.

The planned recovery week after the sand at Rainbow Beach. Wednesday's run moved to Friday after a heavy day on the tools. Saturday's long run was lost to being up all night with the one-year-old. Sunday it happened on the treadmill instead, 5.65km.

Say this
"I missed the long run because I was up all night with my one-year-old. That is going to happen again, probably a lot, and there is no version of this where it does not."
"The rule we settled on is that missed sessions get dropped, not made up. Chasing them is how you end up injured."
Closing beat: this is what training around a young family actually looks like.
Week 417 to 23 August

Vanuatu, and running on hotel treadmills

Run14.0km
Sessions3
LiftingNone

Work trip to Vanuatu from the 17th to the 21st, flagged weeks in advance. The week was deliberately reduced to easy running and bodyweight work only, with lifting suspended until he got home. Two of the three runs were on a belt. Saturday was the first run back, kept to 4km on purpose.

Say this
"Four weeks in and I have had the flu, a work trip overseas, and a night with no sleep. The plan has already been rewritten three times and we are barely started."
"The trip was in the plan before it happened. That is the difference between a plan and a wish list."
Closing beat: the base is being built in the gaps, not in perfect weeks.
Week 524 to 30 August

The week the data turned out to be wrong

Run26.6km
Sessions5 of 5
Max HR190 → 200

The biggest week of the block, and the one where nearly everything got corrected.

  • The course was finally measured. The official map file had been on the race website the whole time. It says 10.04km, 255m of climbing, and one decisive climb of 800m at 9.8% starting at the 4.6km mark. Not at the end, as had been assumed and never checked.
  • Four invented claims were deleted from the plan, including a hill nickname and a description of the finish, none of which had a source.
  • Max heart rate went from 190 to 200, threshold from 172 to 182, after his own 2024 race file showed him averaging 198 for an entire kilometre. Easy running got 10 beats more room overnight.
  • Point Cartwright was measured on Saturday and turned out to be 17m of rise, 205m long, about a minute of climbing. A quarter of what the race asks for.
  • Sunday's easy trail run with his dad turned out to be the answer. The graveyard climbs 870m at 7.3% for 63m of rise. The race climb is 800m at 9.8% for 78m. Same length, four fifths of the vertical.
Say this
"The map file was on the race website the entire time. Nobody opened it. The climb that decides this race is at four point six kilometres, not at the end where everyone assumes."
"My maximum heart rate was wrong by ten beats, which means every easy run I had done was slower than it needed to be."
"The most useful thing I did all week was an easy jog with my dad where we talked the whole way. Not because of the training. Because of what it measured."
Closing beat: five weeks of training built on numbers that had never been checked.
Week 631 August to 6 September

The week he read the plan and found it was wrong

Target29.6km
Sprints7, no fade
Bugs found86

Tuesday's hill sprints: seven 20-second reps at 3:31, 3:08, 3:12, 3:02, 3:14, 3:06 and 3:07 per kilometre. Rep seven as fast as rep two, and the only slow one was the first. Then he opened the week 8 time trial card, saw it prescribing hill sprints, and asked why.

  • Eighteen quality sessions were carrying the wrong brief: all three time trials, six sub-threshold sessions, five VO2 sessions and four rep sessions.
  • A full proof-read of all 198 sessions found 86 problems, including a race-day card that still claimed the course had never been verified, and a race-eve shakeout that was a 7km night trail run with 300m of climbing.
  • The time trial marker moved down from 25:45 to 25:15 after reading interval data from the week before that had never been opened.
  • Trophies confirmed as top three male and top three female only. Third male has been 56:09, 59:40 and 50:23 across three years, so sub-50 would have won one every time.
Say this
"I opened my week eight time trial and it told me to do hill sprints. During a five kilometre time trial. So I asked the question, and eighty-six mistakes fell out of the plan."
"My coach set my marker at twenty-five forty-five, then went and read a session from the week before and moved it to twenty-five fifteen. I would rather have a harder number that is right."
Closing beat: the plan is only as good as the last person who actually read it.

How to end the piece

Do not end on a summary. End on the open question, because that is what makes people come back:

"Six weeks in. Fifteenth of September I run a five kilometre time trial, and the number it gives me decides whether sub-fifty is still on. I have no idea what it is going to say."
